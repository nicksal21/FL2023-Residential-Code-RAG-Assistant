# Electrical Basics and Jobsite Safety

> Sample reference material for the RAG pipeline demo. General construction concepts only — not an official building code.

## Residential Electrical Overview

Residential wiring distributes power from the service entrance through a main panel to branch circuits. Circuits are protected by breakers or fuses sized to the wire gauge and expected load. Grounding and bonding reduce shock risk and help clear faults quickly.

## Common Circuit Types

### Lighting and Receptacle Circuits

General-purpose 120-volt circuits supply lighting and outlets. Kitchen, bathroom, and outdoor areas often have additional requirements for ground-fault and arc-fault protection because of moisture exposure or cord-connected loads.

### Dedicated Appliances

Large appliances such as ranges, dryers, and HVAC equipment may require dedicated circuits with appropriate voltage and amperage. Dedicated lines reduce nuisance tripping and simplify troubleshooting.

## Conduit and Cable Methods

Non-metallic sheathed cable (often referred to by a common trade name) is widely used in wood-framed residential work. Conduit may be required in exposed locations, garages, or where physical damage is likely. All methods must respect fill limits, bend radii, and support spacing.

## Jobsite Safety

### Personal Protective Equipment

Hard hats, eye protection, and appropriate footwear are baseline expectations on active construction sites. Hearing protection is recommended when operating or working near loud equipment.

### Fall Protection

Falls are a leading cause of construction injuries. Guardrails, personal fall-arrest systems, and proper ladder setup reduce risk when working at elevation. Ladders should extend past the landing surface and be set at a stable angle.

### Electrical Safety

Assume all boxes and conductors are live until tested. Use GFCI protection for temporary power on wet sites. Lockout/tagout procedures apply when servicing permanently energized equipment.

## When to Consult a Licensed Professional

Structural, electrical, plumbing, and energy-compliance work should be designed and installed to meet applicable local regulations. This demo corpus illustrates retrieval concepts only; real projects require licensed professionals and authority-having-jurisdiction review.
