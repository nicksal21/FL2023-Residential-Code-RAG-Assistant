# Framing and Structural Systems

> Sample reference material for the RAG pipeline demo. General construction concepts only — not an official building code.

## Load Paths

Residential structures transfer loads from the roof and floors down through walls, headers, and posts to the foundation. Understanding load paths helps framers size members and place supports correctly. Gravity loads include dead load (permanent materials) and live load (occupants, furniture, snow where applicable).

## Wall Framing

### Stud Layout

Wood-framed walls typically use 2x4 or 2x6 studs at 16 or 24 inches on center. Plates at the top and bottom of the wall tie studs together. Openings for doors and windows require headers sized for the span and load above.

### Sheathing

Structural sheathing (often OSB or plywood) applied to exterior walls provides racking resistance against wind and earthquake forces. Sheathing panels are commonly staggered at joints and nailed per manufacturer schedules.

## Roof Framing

### Rafters and Trusses

Site-built rafters and factory-built trusses are both common. Trusses are engineered for specific spans and loads and should not be field-modified without re-evaluation. Collar ties and ridge boards or ridge beams serve different roles in resisting outward thrust.

### Roof Covering Support

Roof decking (typically plywood or OSB) spans rafters or truss chords and supports underlayment and final roofing material. Proper fastening and edge spacing reduce blow-off risk in high winds.

## Connections

Metal connectors — hurricane ties, joist hangers, and hold-downs — supplement nailed joints at critical interfaces. Consistent fastener schedules matter as much as member sizing for overall structural performance.

## Inspection Mindset

Framing inspections often verify bearing points, header sizes, fire blocking, and braced wall lines. Documenting as-built conditions with photos before concealment saves time during later trades and inspections.
