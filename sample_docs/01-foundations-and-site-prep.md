# Foundations and Site Preparation

> Sample reference material for the RAG pipeline demo. General construction concepts only — not an official building code.

## Soil and Grading

Before pouring a foundation, the site should be graded so water drains away from the structure. Standing water near a foundation increases hydrostatic pressure and can cause settlement or basement moisture problems. A common rule of thumb is a minimum slope of 6 inches fall over the first 10 feet away from the footing.

## Foundation Types

### Slab-on-Grade

A slab-on-grade foundation is a single concrete pour that serves as both the floor and the structural base. It is common in warmer climates where frost depth is shallow. A vapor barrier and compacted gravel base are typically placed beneath the slab to control moisture migration.

### Crawl Space

A crawl space elevates the floor system above grade, creating an accessible void for plumbing, electrical, and mechanical runs. Crawl spaces require ventilation or conditioning strategies to limit condensation and mold growth.

### Full Basement

A full basement extends habitable or storage space below grade. Waterproofing, drainage boards, and sump systems are often used where the water table is high or seasonal flooding is a concern.

## Footing Basics

Spread footings distribute structural loads over a wider area of soil. Footing width and depth depend on soil bearing capacity, load from the structure, and local frost-line requirements. Undisturbed soil or properly compacted fill should support footings; organic topsoil is generally removed first.

## Reinforcement

Concrete foundations commonly use steel rebar or welded wire mesh to resist tensile and shear forces. Control joints and expansion joints help manage shrinkage cracking in slabs and walls.
